## Asking who may attend an event

Use when Tym is asking a contact to identify someone from a particular team who is likely to attend an event. This is Tym's preferred wording:

> Any chance you might know who from the sales engineering team is likely to be there?

It works because it asks the complete question directly without assuming the contact has an attendee list or owes Tym an introduction. Replace the team and event reference only from supplied context. Do not pad it with a conditional opening, a second politeness clause or vague language such as `point me in the right direction` or `identify the appropriate stakeholder`. Do not claim that a named person will attend unless the context confirms it.

The call is a real option; a suggestion of others is an optional extension, not the whole point. Use only the actual history, role and relevant context supplied for the current recipient. Do not copy the wording, career areas or company.

Acknowledge the response once, then offer the supplied in-person availability and booking link. Keep a remote option separate. Do not ask `What day and time would suit you?` when useful availability or booking options are already supplied.

> Hi Katrina,
>
> Great to hear from you.
>

Use only the supplied availability and links. If in-person availability or its link is missing, offer the available call option instead.
