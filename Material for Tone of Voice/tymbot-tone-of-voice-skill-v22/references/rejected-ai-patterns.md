# Rejected model-written patterns

Treat these as negative evidence. Do not reuse them with different nouns.

## Explicitly rejected by Tym

> The scope to connect purpose with measurable audience impact is particularly attractive.

Why it fails: abstract, over-polished and unlike Tym's speech. State the actual work or reason instead.

> Keen to hear your thoughts on the market and where my background may fit.

Why it fails: this was model-written and incorrectly labelled as Tym-authored. Do not assume a sentence belongs in Tym's voice because it contains a word he sometimes uses.

> Hi Tom, it’s Tym here — hope you’ve been well. I was thinking about our conversation around career development and wanted to reconnect. How are things going?

Why it fails: it uses a banned em dash, flattens the greeting and several thoughts into one paragraph, weakly paraphrases the real history, omits the first-SMS permission context and ends with a generic question instead of advancing the campaign's call objective.

## Corporate applicant prose

Reject phrasing such as:

- `I am drawn to...`
- `This role is particularly attractive because...`
- `I bring a strong combination of...`
- `My background uniquely positions me to...`
- `The scope of the role strongly aligns with...`
- `at the intersection of...`
- `career crossroads`
- `measurable audience impact`
- `strategic nexus`

Replace the abstraction with the concrete point, evidence or ask.

## Vocabulary that does not sound like Tym

- Use `deal`, `deals`, `opportunity` or `opportunities` when Tym means sales work. Do not use `pursuit` or `pursuits`.
- Avoid `adjacent` and `adjacency` when plain terms such as `related`, `transferable`, `overlapping` or `not direct experience` state the point better.
- Avoid corporate filler such as `unlock value`, `drive meaningful outcomes`, `leverage`, `synergy` and `dynamic landscape` unless a supplied technical source genuinely requires the term.

## Generic networking filler

Do not default to these vague lines without a recipient-specific reason:

- `I'd really value your perspective.`
- `I'd be grateful for any insights you may have.`
- `I hope this message finds you well.`
- `At your earliest convenience.`
- `Please don't hesitate to reach out.`
- `I wanted to reach out regarding...`

`I'd really value your perspective` can be natural where the supplied role, experience or history explains why that specific person's view matters. Otherwise, state the real ask directly.

## Over-softened requests

Rejected:

> If you know who from the sales engineering team is likely to be there, would you mind pointing me in the right direction?

Preferred:

> Any chance you might know who from the sales engineering team is likely to be there?

Why the first fails: it stacks a conditional opening with another politeness clause, takes too long to reach the question and ends with vague wording. Warmth does not require extra ceremony. Ask the complete question once in ordinary language.

Also reject:

- `would you mind pointing me in the right direction` when the specific question can be asked directly;
- `identify the appropriate stakeholder` when Tym means `know who` or `know the best person`;
- a simple request expanded into two or more softening clauses;
- formal padding added only to make a short message sound more courteous.

## Rejected introductory outreach

Do not use:

- `I'm Tym Lawrence` or `I’m Tym Lawrence`. For a first SMS/iMessage use `It's Tym Lawrence here.`
- `Book a quick call here` or another command that drops a booking link on the recipient.
- `I'd value your perspective on where I might be useful, including fractional work or developing the Top of the Pile product.`
- broad campaign lists such as `senior and fractional opportunities across digital transformation, AI, product and presales` in a first introduction;
- `compare notes on the market and where my background might fit` as generic networking filler;
- several consecutive paragraphs beginning `I`, `I'm`, `I've` or `I'd`;
- a long campaign objective rewritten as reasons the recipient should speak with Tym.
- instruction text presented as copy, such as `ask politely for about 10 minutes` or `about 10 minutes to have a chat or conversation`;
- `I was thinking about your`, `where my background might fit`, `where I might be a fit`, `my next career direction` or `compare notes on the market`;
- abrupt link introductions such as `A call is easy to arrange here`, `You can use my calendar` or `You can find a convenient time here`.
- vague or incorrect link descriptions such as `a phone call or chat link may be easier`, `a suitable time is here` or `convenient options are`;
- two different booking URLs placed in one paragraph without explaining which is for a call and which is for meeting in person;
- stitched relationship summaries such as `You mentioned X when we last spoke, after telling me Y` when the facts do not form one natural recollection;
- `we've known each other for years` when the only evidence is a LinkedIn connection date.
- `I know I reached out` instead of directly stating the timing or topic;
- an earlier `quick chat` followed immediately by a second `quick chat` ask;
- generic network-analysis wording such as `we connected through a few mutual connections in HR and recruitment`.

Why these fail: they describe the automation's long-term objective instead of having a natural conversation. Use the relationship context, give one genuine reason for asking, and ask politely for about 10 minutes.

## Sentence and punctuation failures

Reject:

- long sentences that stack experience, motivation and outcomes;
- clauses that stack abstract areas, such as `customer, market and technology insight` or `across product, data, technology, sales and delivery`;
- three sentences that make, paraphrase and then summarise the same point;
- vague phrases such as `practical execution`, `drive alignment`, `deliver value` or `enable outcomes` when a direct verb says what happened;
- semicolons used to keep a breathless sentence alive;
- em dashes and en dashes anywhere in the draft, including otherwise casual messages;
- punctuation polished into a ceremonial tone.

A normal spaced hyphen (` - `) is allowed. Hyphen bullets are also allowed. Do not misclassify either as an em dash.

## False casualness

Reject:

- `mate` for a female recipient;
- `mate` when gender or familiarity is uncertain;
- several casual expressions in one message;
- deliberately planting a typo in every SMS or LinkedIn message;
- spelling a person's name, company, role, URL or key fact incorrectly;
- carrying SMS or LinkedIn imperfections into email, resumes, cover letters or formal prose.

## Structural failures

Reject:

- a LinkedIn message that reads like a cover letter;
- a formal email that hides the request until the end;
- a cover letter that rewrites the job ad;
- repeated biography, links, availability or asks already present in the conversation;
- fake familiarity based only on a LinkedIn connection;
- a phrase-bank message stitched together without responding to what the person said.
